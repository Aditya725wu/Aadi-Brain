# Signal to Noise Ratio (SNR)

## Definition

SNR indicates the strength of the signal relative to the noise power in
the system.

It is a ratio of two powers.

\[ `\boxed{SNR=P_s/P_n}`{=tex} \]

Usually SNR is expressed in decibels:

\[ `\boxed{SNR_{dB}=10\log_{10}(SNR)}`{=tex} \]

where: - (P_s) = signal power - (P_n) = noise power

## Interpretation

Higher SNR means the signal is stronger relative to noise.

Lower SNR means the channel is noisier relative to the signal.

## Sample

**Q. A signal has power 10 mW and noise power 1 μW. Find SNR and SNR in
dB.**

Write:

``` text
Given:
Ps = 10 mW
Pn = 1 μW = 0.001 mW

SNR = Ps/Pn
Then convert SNR to dB.
```
