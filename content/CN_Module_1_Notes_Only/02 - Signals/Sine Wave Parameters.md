# Sine Wave Parameters

A simple periodic analog signal is a **sine wave**.

A sine wave can be described using four characteristics:

1. Amplitude
2. Frequency
3. Period
4. Phase

---

## 1. Amplitude

Amplitude is the **maximum value of the signal's strength**.

It indicates the magnitude/height of the signal.

A larger amplitude means a stronger signal.

---

## 2. Frequency

Frequency is the **rate of change with respect to time**.

It is the number of cycles completed per second.

Unit: **Hertz (Hz)**.

### Formula

> **FORMULA**
>
> `f = 1/T`

High frequency → rapid change.

Low frequency → slow change.

---

## 3. Period

Period is the **amount of time required to complete one cycle**.

Unit: seconds.

### Formula

> **FORMULA**
>
> `T = 1/f`

---

## 4. Phase

Phase describes the **position of the waveform relative to time 0**.

Two waves can have the same frequency and amplitude but different phases.

## Time Domain and Frequency Domain

A sine wave can be represented in:

- Time domain
- Frequency domain

A complete sine wave in the time domain can be represented by a **single spike** in the frequency domain.

## Important Relationship

Amplitude changes the magnitude/height of the frequency-domain spike.

Frequency determines the position of the spike on the frequency axis.
