# Time Domain and Frequency Domain

The same signal can be represented in two different ways.

## 1. Time Domain

In the **time domain**, the horizontal axis represents **time** and the vertical axis represents **amplitude**.

It shows how the signal changes with time.

`Amplitude vs Time`

### What can we see?

- Wave shape
- Amplitude
- Period
- Timing of changes

---

## 2. Frequency Domain

In the **frequency domain**, the horizontal axis represents **frequency** and the vertical axis represents **amplitude**.

It shows the frequency components contained in a signal.

`Amplitude vs Frequency`

For a pure sine wave of frequency `f`, the frequency-domain representation is one spike at `f`.

## Example

A sine wave with frequency **6 Hz**:

- Time domain → continuous sine waveform
- Frequency domain → one spike at 6 Hz

A composite signal containing 8 Hz, 16 Hz and another frequency component can be represented by separate spikes at those frequencies.

## Why Frequency Domain Is Useful

When a signal contains many sine waves, the time-domain waveform can become complicated.

The frequency domain gives a compact representation by showing the individual frequency components.

## Important Point

**Time domain = how the signal changes with time.**

**Frequency domain = which frequencies are present and their amplitudes.**

## Periodic and Nonperiodic

- Periodic composite signal → discrete frequency components
- Nonperiodic composite signal → continuous range of frequencies
