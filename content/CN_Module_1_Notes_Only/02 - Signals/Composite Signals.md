# Composite Signals

## Definition

A **composite signal** is a signal made from multiple simple sine waves.

A single-frequency sine wave is not useful for carrying typical data by itself.

Data communication generally uses composite signals.

## Fourier Analysis

According to Fourier analysis, a composite signal can be represented as a combination of simple sine waves with different:

- Frequencies
- Amplitudes
- Phases

Therefore, a complicated signal can be analyzed as several simpler sine-wave components.

## Example

Suppose a composite signal contains:

- Frequency `f`
- Frequency `3f`
- Frequency `9f`

In the frequency domain, these components appear as three spikes:

`f`, `3f`, `9f`

The height of each spike represents the corresponding component's amplitude.

## Frequency Domain

For a composite signal, the frequency domain is often more compact and useful because it directly shows the individual frequency components.

## Exam Definition

> A composite signal is a signal formed by the combination of multiple simple sine waves having different frequencies, amplitudes, and phases.
