# Attenuation

## Definition

**Attenuation** means loss of energy as a signal travels through a transmission medium.

The signal becomes **weaker**.

## Why Does It Occur?

When a signal travels through a medium, it loses energy while overcoming the resistance of the medium.

## Compensation

**Amplifiers** are used to compensate for loss of energy by amplifying the signal.

## Measurement of Attenuation

Attenuation or gain is measured using **decibels (dB)**.

> **FORMULA**
>
> `dB = 10 log10(P2/P1)`

where:

- `P1` = input signal power
- `P2` = output signal power

### Interpretation

If `P2 < P1`:

`P2/P1 < 1`

Therefore, dB value is negative → **loss/attenuation**.

If `P2 > P1`:

dB value is positive → **gain**.

## Numerical Example

Given:

`P1 = 10 W`

`P2 = 1 W`

`dB = 10 log10(1/10)`

`= 10 log10(0.1)`

`≈ -10 dB`

Therefore, the signal has a **10 dB loss**.

## Special Point

Half the power corresponds approximately to:

> **-3 dB**
